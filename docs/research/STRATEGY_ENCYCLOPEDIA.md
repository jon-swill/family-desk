# Strategy encyclopedia

Every common family of retail-accessible strategy and system, with how it works, why it might work, what usually kills it, and whether it fits this desk. Written from general knowledge on 2026-11-24; **every "Evidence" cell must be re-verified against primary sources in Phase 1** before a strategy enters Phase 4 testing.

Fit key: **Core** = can sit in the long-term bucket · **Sleeve** = active sleeve candidate · **Watch** = study only · **No** = not viable for this desk (latency, cost, legal, or options-manual rule).

## 1. Trend following

| Strategy | Typical rules | Why it might work | Evidence and decay | Main failure mode | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SMA crossover (e.g. 50/200 "golden/death cross") | Long when fast SMA > slow SMA; flat or defensive otherwise | Slow-moving capital and behavioral underreaction create persistent trends | [Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1992.tb04681.x): moving-average and trading-range-break rules on the Dow 1897–1986 gave higher, less volatile returns after buy signals. Out-of-sample: [Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00163) found the best rule "not even statistically significant" in 1987–1996 and no rule beat the benchmark on S&P 500 futures. Drawdown-reduction claim unverified | Whipsaw in sideways markets; late exits and entries | Core overlay / Sleeve |
| Price vs long moving average (e.g. 10-month / 200-day filter) | Hold asset when price above its MA, else T-bills | Same; avoids the worst of bear markets historically | [Faber (2007)](https://doi.org/10.3905/jwm.2007.674809) (originator, J. Wealth Mgmt): 10-month SMA timing tested in-sample on US equities and out-of-sample on 20+ markets; abstract reports "equity-like returns with bond-like volatility and drawdown" since 1972. Post-publication results and the "lower return in bull markets" claim unverified (author's later SSRN update not reachable) | Whipsaw; tax drag from realized gains | Core overlay |
| Time-series momentum (12-month return sign) | Long assets with positive trailing return, scaled by volatility | Trend persistence across asset classes | [Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003): 1–12 month persistence in all 58 futures studied, best in extreme markets. Challenge: [Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.08.004) find "little evidence of TSM" asset by asset, in- and out-of-sample, with profits matching a no-predictability sample-mean strategy. [Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen (2017)](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2017.44.1.015) extend the test to 1880 and discuss higher cross-market correlations since the credit crisis. "Weaker 2010s" not quantified in the sources read | Sharp reversals; crowding | Sleeve (ETF-based) |
| Dual momentum | Pick the stronger of US vs international equity; go to bonds if both trail T-bills | Combines relative and absolute momentum | unverified (originator paper [Antonacci (2012)](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2042750) exists but SSRN blocks automated access, so its abstract was not read; no independent out-of-sample study read) | Few signals, long flat periods, concentration | Sleeve |
| Donchian / turtle channel breakout | Buy new N-day high, exit on M-day low | Captures large trends; famous turtle rules | [Szakmary, Shen & Sharma (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2009.08.004): dual-MA and channel rules on 28 commodity futures over 48 years earned positive mean excess returns net of costs in at least 22 of 28 markets, robust to data-mining adjustments. Survey [Park & Irwin (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-6419.2007.00519.x): early studies found profits in FX and futures "but not in stock markets"; modern studies profitable "at least until the early 1990s". Turtle rules themselves: no primary study | Many false breakouts; long losing streaks | Sleeve (ETFs) |
| Trend pullback | In an uptrend (price > 200-day), buy a dip to the 20/50-day MA or an RSI dip; exit on new high or trend break | Buy weakness within strength | unverified (practitioner setup; no primary study found) | Pullback becomes a trend reversal | Sleeve |
| ADX-filtered trend | Trade trend signals only when ADX shows a strong trend | Avoid choppy regimes | unverified (originator is Wilder's 1978 book, not online; no peer-reviewed test of ADX filtering found) | Lag; over-filtering | Watch |
| MACD signal-line cross | Long when MACD crosses above signal | Momentum change detection | [Chong & Ng (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1080/13504850600993598): MACD and RSI rules beat buy-and-hold "in most cases" on 60 years of the FT30; follow-up [Chong, Ng & Liew (2014)](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm7010001) found MACD(12,26,0) significant only in Milan and the TSX among five other OECD indices. Neither abstract addresses costs or data snooping; the "weak after costs" claim is not confirmed by these sources | Noise, whipsaw | Watch (as a feature) |
| Parabolic SAR / Supertrend / chandelier exits | Trailing-stop-based trend systems | Lock in trend gains | unverified (practitioner trailing-stop tools; Wilder's 1978 book originates Parabolic SAR, not online; no peer-reviewed test found) | Stopped out by normal volatility | Exit tool |

## 2. Momentum (cross-sectional) and rotation

| Strategy | Typical rules | Why it might work | Evidence and decay | Main failure mode | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12-1 stock momentum | Buy top decile by 12-month return excluding last month; rebalance monthly | Underreaction, herding | [Jegadeesh & Titman (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x): winners-minus-losers earn significant returns over 3–12 month holds, partly reversing in years 2–3. Out-of-sample: [Jegadeesh & Titman (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00342) found profits "continued in the 1990s". Crashes: [Daniel & Moskowitz (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2015.12.002) show crashes occur in panic states and coincide with market rebounds. General decay: [McLean & Pontiff (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365) find published predictors' returns 58% lower post-publication. "Weaker recently" for momentum specifically not quantified in the sources read | Momentum crashes at market rebounds; turnover | Sleeve (via ETF or small basket) |
| Sector / industry rotation | Hold the top 2–3 sector ETFs by 3–12 month momentum | Industry momentum | [Moskowitz & Grinblatt (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00146): industry momentum "appear[s] highly profitable" and explains much of stock momentum, after controls for size, book-to-market and microstructure. The "modest after costs" claim is not addressed in the abstract (unverified); post-publication decay not measured in the sources read | Rotation lag; concentration | Sleeve |
| Asset-class rotation (GTAA) | Rotate among stocks, bonds, gold, REITs, cash by momentum/trend | Diversified trend capture | [Faber (2007)](https://doi.org/10.3905/jwm.2007.674809) (originator, J. Wealth Mgmt): trend timing applied to an S&P 500 / EAFE / GSCI / REIT / 10-year Treasury mix since 1972 gave "equity-like returns with bond-like volatility and drawdown". No independent post-publication study read; "variable returns" unverified | Long underperformance vs equities in bull runs | Core overlay / Sleeve |
| 52-week-high proximity | Buy stocks near 52-week highs | Anchoring bias | [George & Hwang (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00695.x): nearness to the 52-week high explains a large portion of momentum profits, dominates past returns as a forecaster, and does not reverse long-run. No post-publication study read; subject to the general decay in [McLean & Pontiff (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365) | Same crash risk as momentum | Watch |
| Earnings momentum / revisions | Buy stocks with rising analyst estimates | Slow information diffusion | [Chan, Jegadeesh & Lakonishok (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb05222.x): past earnings surprises predict drifts independently of price momentum, and analysts' forecasts "respond sluggishly" to past news. No post-publication study read; data-cost claim not checked | Data access; crowding | Watch |

## 3. Mean reversion

| Strategy | Typical rules | Why it might work | Evidence and decay | Main failure mode | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RSI(2) reversion on index ETFs | In an uptrend, buy when 2-day RSI < 10; sell when RSI > 70 or close > 5-day MA | Short-term overreaction in liquid indices | unverified (originator is Connors & Alvarez's 2008 book, not online; no peer-reviewed test found; decay claim unsourced) | Buying into a crash; small edge eaten by costs | Sleeve |
| RSI dip (14-period < 30) | Buy oversold, sell on recovery | Overreaction | [Chong, Ng & Liew (2014)](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm7010001): RSI(14, 30/70) was profitable on the Dow Jones Industrials and RSI(21,50) in Milan and the TSX (index-level tests; abstract silent on costs). The single-stock and trend-filter claims are unverified | "Oversold" keeps falling | Sleeve (with trend filter) |
| Bollinger Band reversion | Buy at lower band, sell at middle/upper | Volatility-scaled overreaction | [Lento, Gradojević & Wright (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1080/17446540701206576): after transaction costs Bollinger Bands were "consistently unable" to beat buy-and-hold; a contrarian version improved profitability. "Works in ranges, fails in trends" unverified | Band walks in strong trends | Sleeve |
| Internal bar strength / close-near-low | Buy index ETF closing near day's low | Short-term reversal | [Pandey & Joshi (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12434) (non-peer-reviewed arXiv report): IBS mean-reversion on a basket of country ETFs over 10 years was "a useful technical indicator". No peer-reviewed or out-of-sample study found | Same as above | Watch |
| Short-term reversal (1-week/1-month losers) | Buy last week's/month's losers | Liquidity provision | [Jegadeesh (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb05110.x) (monthly) and [Lehmann (1990)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2937816) (weekly) document reversals; Lehmann reports profits persisting after bid-ask and plausible costs. Later: [Avramov, Chordia & Goyal (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2006.01060.x) find reversal profits concentrated in illiquid stocks and "smaller than the likely transactions costs". "Captured by fast players" unverified | Costs, news-driven drops that don't revert | Watch |
| Gap fade | Fade overnight gaps that are not news-driven | Opening overreaction | unverified (no primary study found) | Gaps with real news keep going | No (open-auction risk, R-24) |
| VWAP reversion intraday | Trade back toward VWAP | Intraday overextension | [Berkowitz, Logue & Noser (1988)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1988.tb02591.x) introduced the day's VWAP as a measure of execution cost on NYSE trades, which supports its role as an execution benchmark. No primary study of VWAP reversion as an alpha source found (unverified) | HFT competition | No |

## 4. Breakout and volatility expansion

| Strategy | Typical rules | Why it might work | Evidence and decay | Main failure mode | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Range breakout (N-day high) | Buy close above recent range high with volume | New information, trend start | [Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1992.tb04681.x) found trading-range-break buy signals beat sell signals on the Dow 1897–1986, but [Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00163) found no rule from that universe significant out-of-sample in 1987–1996. "Most breakouts fail" unverified | False breakouts | Sleeve (with filters) |
| Opening range breakout | Trade break of first 15–30 min range | Intraday momentum | [Zarattini & Aziz (2023)](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4416622) (practitioner SSRN paper, not peer reviewed): an ORB strategy on QQQ 2016–2023 reported large alpha net of commissions, with headline results relying on leverage or 3x leveraged ETFs (blocked instruments here). A [2025 replication](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5325376) exists but its abstract was not reachable; cost sensitivity unverified | Chop; slippage | Watch (day trading, R-36) |
| Volatility contraction (squeeze) | Enter when Bollinger width / ATR hits multi-month low and then expands | Calm before moves | unverified (practitioner; Bollinger's own book is the originator, not online; no peer-reviewed test found) | Direction ambiguity | Watch |
| Cup-and-handle / chart patterns | Pattern recognition | Behavioral | [Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00265): automated kernel-regression detection of patterns (head-and-shoulders, double bottoms, etc.) on US stocks 1962–1996 found "several technical indicators do provide incremental information and may have some practical value"; trading profits after costs were not shown in the abstract, and cup-and-handle was not among the patterns tested in the abstract | Pattern ambiguity, overfitting | No (unless rules are fully mechanical) |

## 5. Volatility, risk-based and allocation systems

| Strategy | Typical rules | Why it might work | Evidence and decay | Main failure mode | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic index allocation + band rebalancing | Fixed weights; rebalance when drift exceeds bands | Low cost, diversification, disciplined buy-low/sell-high | [Brinson, Hood & Beebower (1986)](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v42.n4.39): in 91 US pension plans 1974–83, investment policy explained on average 93.6% of the variation in total return, and active management cost 1.10% a year versus the policy benchmark. [Vanguard, Jaconetti, Kinniry & Zilbering (2010)](https://indexacapital.com/bundles/unaiadvisor/docs/papers/2010-Vanguard-Best-practices-for-portfolio-rebalancing.pdf) (third-party copy; no live Vanguard URL found): no optimal frequency or threshold; annual or semiannual monitoring with 5% thresholds balances risk control and cost. "Strongest of anything here" is a judgement, not a sourced claim | Behavior (abandoning it) | Core (the fee replacement) |
| Volatility targeting | Scale exposure inversely to recent volatility | Volatility clusters; risk-adjusted returns improve | [Moreira & Muir (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12513): volatility-managed factor portfolios produce large alphas and higher Sharpe ratios in spanning regressions. Out-of-sample challenge: [Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.04.015), across 103 equity strategies, find volatility-managed portfolios "do not systematically outperform" and realistic real-time versions generally earn lower Sharpe ratios than the unmanaged portfolios | De-risking just before rebounds | Core overlay / Sleeve |
| Risk parity | Weight assets by inverse volatility/risk contribution | Balanced risk | [Asness, Frazzini & Pedersen (2012)](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v68.n1.1): safer assets offer higher risk-adjusted returns, and harvesting them "requires leverage"; risk parity overweights safer assets. Unlevered performance and post-publication results not in the sources read | Rate shocks hit bonds and stocks together (2022) | Watch (no leverage) |
| Minimum variance / low volatility | Hold lowest-volatility stocks/ETF | Low-vol anomaly | [Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2006.00836.x): high idiosyncratic-volatility stocks have "abysmally low" returns; [Baker, Bradley & Wurgler (2011)](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v67.n1.4): low-beta and low-volatility stocks have long outperformed, linked to benchmark-constrained arbitrage; [Frazzini & Pedersen (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.10.005): betting-against-beta factor earns significant risk-adjusted returns across markets. Post-publication decay and ETF availability not checked | Rate sensitivity, crowding | Core tilt |
| CPPI / drawdown control | Exposure = multiple × (value − floor) | Guarantees a floor in theory | [Perold & Sharpe (1988)](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v44.n1.16): CPPI is a form of portfolio insurance with "better downside protection and better upside potential than buy-and-hold" (abstract truncated at the source just before the stated trade-off). Theory: [Black & Perold (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0165-1889(92)90043-E) (abstract not reachable). No out-of-sample study read | Gap risk; locks into cash after a drop | Watch |
| Kelly / fractional Kelly sizing | Size by edge/variance | Growth-optimal | [Kelly (1956)](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1956.tb03809.x): maximizing log wealth gives the maximum exponential growth rate. [MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba (2010)](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf) ("Good and bad properties of the Kelly criterion"): Kelly bets "may be very large" and risky short-term; given "extreme sensitivity" to errors in mean estimates, wagers should be reduced; fractional Kelly trades growth for security | Overbetting on bad estimates | Sizing tool (fractional only) |

## 6. Factor and fundamental

| Strategy | Typical rules | Why it might work | Evidence and decay | Main failure mode | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value (cheap on P/B, P/E, EV/EBIT) | Buy cheapest quintile | Risk premium / behavioral | [Fama & French (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1992.tb04398.x): size and book-to-market capture the cross-section of average returns. Decay: [Fama & French (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1093/rapstu/raaa021) find value premiums "much lower in the second half" of July 1963–June 2019, though volatility prevents rejecting equal expected premiums in both halves. The specific 2007–2020 window was not checked in a source | Value traps; long droughts | Core tilt via ETF |
| Quality / profitability | Buy high ROE, stable margins, low leverage | Underpriced quality | [Novy-Marx (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.01.003): gross profits-to-assets predicts returns about as well as book-to-market, and controlling for it improves value strategies. [Asness, Frazzini & Pedersen (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-018-9470-2) (quality minus junk): significant risk-adjusted returns in the US and 24 countries; the price of quality varies over time and a low price predicts higher returns. No post-publication study read | Expensive in some regimes | Core tilt via ETF |
| Multi-factor (value + momentum + quality) | Composite score | Diversified factor premia | [Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12021): value and momentum premia in eight markets and asset classes, negatively correlated with each other. Decay: [McLean & Pontiff (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365), 97 predictors, returns 26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower post-publication, with post-publication rises in correlation with other published predictors (consistent with crowding) | Factor drawdowns | Core tilt / Sleeve |
| Shareholder yield / dividend growth | Buy high payout + buybacks | Capital discipline | [Boudoukh, Michaely, Richardson & Roberts (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2007.01226.x): payout yield (dividends plus repurchases) and net payout yield carry more cross-sectional return information than dividend yield, and the high-minus-low payout portfolio is a priced factor. Academic, not only practitioner; dividend-growth screens and post-publication results not checked | Sector concentration | Core tilt |
| Small-cap premium | Overweight small caps | Size premium | Originator [Banz (1981)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(81)90018-0) (abstract not reachable). Review [van Dijk (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2011.05.009): recent empirical studies assert the size effect "has disappeared after the early 1980s". Counterpoint [Asness, Frazzini, Israel, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.05.006): controlling for quality/junk, a significant and stable size premium re-emerges | Long droughts | Watch |

## 7. Event-driven

| Strategy | Typical rules | Why it might work | Evidence and decay | Main failure mode | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-earnings announcement drift | Buy positive surprises, hold weeks | Slow information diffusion | Documented by [Bernard & Thomas (1989)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2491062) (J. Accounting Research). Much weaker now: [Martineau (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1561/104.00000122) (Critical Finance Review) finds PEAD non-existent in large stocks since 2006 and only recently gone in microcaps; 2025 papers dispute this ([UCLA Anderson Review summary, 2026](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)). Decay base rate: published predictors earn 26% less out-of-sample and 58% less post-publication ([McLean & Pontiff 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365), J. Finance) | Quick reversal; crowding | Sleeve (LLM can read releases) |
| Earnings-call / filing NLP signals | Score tone/changes in 8-K, 10-Q, calls | Information in text | Research literature: finance-specific word lists link 10-K tone to filing returns, volume and volatility ([Loughran & McDonald 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01625.x), J. Finance); firms that change their 10-K/10-Q wording underperform, up to 188 bp/month long-short alpha, with no announcement effect ([Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen 2020, "Lazy Prices"](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12885), J. Finance). LLMs now widespread: GPT-4 headline scores predict returns, more for small stocks and bad news, but strategy returns decline as LLM adoption grows ([Lopez-Lira & Tang 2023, rev. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619)). Only forward tests count (F-09) | Look-ahead in tests; crowding | Sleeve, forward-test only (R-31) |
| Index additions/deletions | Trade around index rebalances | Forced buying/selling | S&P 500 inclusion effect first documented by [Shleifer (1986)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1986.tb04518.x) (J. Finance). Mostly arbitraged away: the addition abnormal return fell from 7.4% in the 1990s to under 1% over the last decade, and deletions averaged 0.1% in 2010–2020 ([Greenwood & Sammon 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13410), J. Finance, online 2024; [NBER w30748](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30748), 2022) | Front-running is crowded | Watch |
| Insider buying (Form 4) | Follow clusters of open-market insider buys | Insiders know more | Documented modest effect: little price movement when insiders trade; predictive power comes from purchases and is concentrated in smaller firms (1975–95, [Lakonishok & Lee 2001](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/14.1.79), RFS). Non-routine ("opportunistic") trades earn 82 bp/month value-weighted abnormal returns, routine trades about zero ([Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16454), J. Finance). Free data from EDGAR. Post-publication decay not separately measured; apply the McLean-Pontiff haircut (R-20) | Small, slow edge | Sleeve |
| Buyback announcements | Buy on new authorizations | Signaling | Documented: 12.1% average abnormal four-year return after 1980–90 announcements, 45.3% for value stocks, none for glamour stocks ([Ikenberry, Lakonishok & Vermaelen 1995](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4965), J. Financial Economics). Decayed: long-run abnormal returns disappear for 2003–2012 events ([Fu & Huang 2016](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2150), Management Science) | Announcements not executed | Watch |
| Merger arbitrage | Buy target after announced deal | Deal-completion premium | Institutional; about 4% a year excess return after transaction costs over 4,750 deals (1963–98), with returns resembling short uncovered index puts and correlated with the market in severe declines, i.e. tail risk if deals break ([Mitchell & Pulvino 2001](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00401), J. Finance) | Deal breaks | Watch |
| 13F / "guru" following | Copy large funds' holdings | Skill transfer | 45-day lag is real: each 13F "is due within 45 days after the end of the calendar quarter" ([SEC Form 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/investment/13ffaq)). That the lag erodes value is unverified (no 13F-specific peer-reviewed study read); related mutual-fund evidence cuts the other way: copycats of disclosed holdings performed comparably to their targets after costs ([Verbeek & Wang 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2013.04.024), J. Banking & Finance) | Stale data | Watch |
| Analyst upgrades / news sentiment | Trade on rating changes or headlines | Information flow | New recommendations move prices ([Womack 1996](https://doi.org/10.2307/2329305), J. Finance), but capturing the drift takes high turnover and abnormal net returns are not reliably above zero after costs ([Barber, Lehavy, McNichols & Trueman 2001](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00336), J. Finance). "Fast players act first" is consistent but not separately verified | Latency | Watch (alerts only) |

## 8. Relative value and statistical arbitrage

| Strategy | Typical rules | Why it might work | Evidence and decay | Main failure mode | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pairs trading (distance / cointegration) | Long the laggard, short the leader when spread widens | Mean reversion of related assets | A simple distance rule earned up to 11% a year in excess returns, 1962–2002 ([Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhj020), RFS). Returns fell, starting earlier than "after 2000s": the top-20-pair mean excess return dropped from 0.86% a month (1962–88) to 0.37% (1989–2002) and 0.24% (2003–09) ([Do & Faff 2010](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v66.n4.1), FAJ) | Pairs break; needs shorting (off) | Watch (needs shorting) |
| ETF vs basket / ADR arbitrage | Trade price vs fair value of components | Arbitrage | Done by HFTs at millisecond speed: mechanical arbitrage between related instruments (e.g. S&P 500 futures vs SPY) is built into continuous markets, and competition "only raised the bar for how fast one has to be to capture them" ([Budish, Cramton & Shim 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjv027), QJE). The AP creation/redemption role is not separately cited | Latency, conversion access | No |
| Closed-end fund discount reversion | Buy funds at unusually wide discounts | Discount mean reversion | Documented niche: discounts move with individual-investor sentiment and narrow when small stocks do well ([Lee, Shleifer & Thaler 1991](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1991.tb03746.x), J. Finance); fund premia are negatively related to future fund returns ([Pontiff 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X%2894%2900800-G), J. Financial Economics; confirmed via abstract index only). No post-publication decay study found | Discounts can widen for years | Watch |
| Market making / pennying (the ETC winner) | Quote both sides near fair value | Capture spread | Real mainly for speed-advantaged firms: a large HFT market maker earned the bid-ask spread but lost on inventory ([Menkveld 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2013.06.006), J. Financial Markets), and the speed race decides who captures the rents ([Budish, Cramton & Shim 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjv027)). Dependence on rebates is unverified (no primary source read) | Adverse selection by faster players | No |

## 9. Seasonality and calendar

| Strategy | Typical rules | Why it might work | Evidence and decay | Main failure mode | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Turn-of-month | Hold index from last trading day to 3rd of next month | Fund flows | Documented over 90 years of Dow data ([Lakonishok & Smidt 1988](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/1.4.403), RFS). "Weaker" is not supported by the sources read: over 1926–2005 the effect was so strong that investors earned no market premium outside the turn of the month, it appears in 31 of 35 countries, and fund flows and volume did not explain it ([McConnell & Xu 2008](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v64.n2.11), FAJ). Decay after 2005 unverified (no later primary study read) | Small edge | Watch |
| Pre-holiday / pre-FOMC drift | Hold index into holidays or FOMC days | Flows / risk premium | Documented then decayed. Pre-holiday: returns 9–14 times normal days, over a third of the market's 1963–82 return ([Ariel 1990](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb03731.x), J. Finance); out of sample (1983–2019) it survives only in small firms and is insignificant for large firms, especially after 1990 ([Ko & Yang 2024](https://doi.org/10.1561/104.00000111), Critical Finance Review). Pre-FOMC: large excess equity returns before FOMC decisions ([Lucca & Moench 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12196), J. Finance), which "essentially disappeared after 2015" ([Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2020.101781), Finance Research Letters) | Small, noisy | Watch |
| "Sell in May" / Halloween | Hold equities Nov–Apr | Seasonality | Mixed; data-mined risk. Originated by [Bouman & Jacobsen (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1257/000282802762024683) (AER); [Maberly & Pierce (2004)](https://econjwatch.org/articles/stock-market-efficiency-withstands-another-challenge-solving-the-sell-in-may-buy-after-halloween-puzzle) (Econ Journal Watch) find the US effect insignificant once the 1987 crash and 1998 LTCM outliers are controlled and "not economically exploitable"; [Zhang & Jacobsen (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jimonfin.2020.102268) (J. Int. Money and Finance) find Nov–Apr returns about 4% higher on average across all available world indices | Missing summer rallies | Watch |
| Tax-loss selling / January effect | Buy December losers | Tax-motivated selling | Mostly faded. Tax-loss selling tested as a cause of small-firm January returns by [Reinganum (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X%2883%2990029-6) (J. Financial Economics; abstract not read). The small-firm turn-of-year effect "became weaker" after publication and does not show up in small-cap practitioners' portfolio returns ([Schwert 2003, NBER w9277](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9277), Handbook of the Economics of Finance) | Small | Watch |

## 12. What the Phase 4 test plan needs for every candidate

1. Pre-registration (R-17): hypothesis, economic rationale, universe, parameters, benchmark, kill criteria.
2. Point-in-time data, survivorship-free universe, realistic costs and slippage.
3. Walk-forward test, then one untouched holdout check (R-18).
4. Trial count and deflated Sharpe ([Bailey & López de Prado 2014](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094), J. Portfolio Management; corrects the Sharpe ratio for selection bias under multiple testing and non-normal returns).
5. Regime breakdown (bull, bear, sideways, high-vol) and worst drawdown.
6. Capacity and turnover; after-tax result vs the core benchmark.
7. Forward-logged paper results before any live money.
